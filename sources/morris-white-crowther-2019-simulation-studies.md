# Source note — Morris, White & Crowther (2019), simulation studies / ADEMP

**Source type:** peer-reviewed methodological tutorial  
**Publication:** *Statistics in Medicine*  
**Citation:** Morris TP, White IR, Crowther MJ. *Using simulation studies to evaluate statistical methods*. 2019;38(11):2074-2102.  
**DOI:** 10.1002/sim.8086  
**PMID:** 30652356  
**PMCID:** PMC6492164  
**Primary URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sim.8086  
**PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30652356/

## Source-supported observations

The paper treats simulation studies as computer experiments in which data are generated by pseudo-random sampling and some truth is known through the data-generating process. That known truth allows investigators to study operating characteristics of statistical methods, including finite-sample behavior.

The paper proposes a structured planning/reporting framework called **ADEMP**:

- **Aims**;
- **Data-generating mechanisms**;
- **Estimands and other targets**;
- **Methods**;
- **Performance measures**.

It discusses simulation studies for, among other purposes:

- checking algebra/code and method implementation;
- evaluating finite-sample behavior where asymptotic theory may be insufficient;
- evaluating a method absolutely;
- comparing alternative methods;
- supporting sample-size or power calculation under assumptions.

It also emphasizes that simulation studies require careful design, execution, analysis and reporting rather than being treated as informal code experiments.

## Harness adaptation boundary

The source does **not** prescribe the Scientific Research Harness lifecycle, Chronicle, authorization boundaries, human-understanding gates, independent reviewer contract, or project-specific freeze semantics.

The harness adaptation proposed in `design/CONDITIONAL_ADEMP_SIMULATION_STUDY_CONTRACT.md` is therefore a local reusable governance rule:

> when a simulation is itself decision-bearing methodological evidence, use ADEMP to make the simulation experiment explicit and prospectively reviewable.

That adaptation must not be represented as a normative requirement from Morris et al.
